# Case 06: Multiple Clinical Failures in Dementia Aged Care — Pressure Injuries, Nutrition, Falls

**Source**: HDC NZ Decision 24HDC00630 (Aged Care Commissioner, 23 February 2026)
**Event Type**: Aged care — multiple concurrent failures
**SAC Equivalent**: SAC 2 (significant harm — pressure injuries, severe weight loss, inadequate post-fall care)
**Difficulty**: 2 — Moderate

## Case Narrative

[Mr A], a 77-year-old man with Alzheimer's disease, hypertension, and chronic kidney disease, was admitted to [Aged Care Facility X] (a private hospital dementia care unit) on 30 January 2023. He was independently mobile at admission and required supervision only for activities of daily living. Over the following 14 months, he experienced significant and preventable deterioration across three clinical domains: pressure injuries, nutritional decline, and falls — each characterised by inadequate nursing assessment, documentation, and escalation.

The Aged Care Commissioner found [Aged Care Facility X] breached Right 4(1) of the Code of Health and Disability Services Consumers' Rights.

**Pressure Injuries (March 2023)**

On 6 March 2023, nursing staff documented [Mr A]'s right heel as "quite red with nil evidence of blood." No skin integrity assessment followed and there was no escalation to clinical management.

Two days later, on 8 March 2023, bilateral heel injuries were identified and documented as "a pressure injury starting." The clinical record revealed critical gaps in response:

- No wound photography was taken
- No specialist wound-care advice was sought
- No wound staging was performed
- No care plan was developed to guide pressure injury management
- No repositioning chart documented turning schedules
- The family ([Mrs B], who held Enduring Power of Attorney) received no notification

The clinical adviser identified "concerns with nursing assessment, timely delivery of wound care, evaluation processes, communication and documentation standards" and characterised this as a moderate-to-serious departure from accepted standards.

**Nutritional Decline and Weight Loss (March 2023 – April 2024)**

[Mr A]'s progressive oral intake decline began from 1 March 2023. Despite this documented decline, no short-term care plan was commenced to guide staff on managing his food and fluid intake.

His weight loss was severe: from 90.6 kg in February 2023 to 76.4 kg by April 2024 — a loss of approximately 13.6 kg over 14 months. Despite this trajectory, there was no evidence that [Mr A]'s weight loss was escalated to care-home leaders, and no increased monitoring was implemented until late in the period.

The clinical adviser characterised the nutritional management as a moderate-to-serious departure from accepted standards.

**Fall and Post-Fall Management (3 March 2023)**

On 3 March 2023, [Mr A] experienced an unwitnessed fall and was found on his bedroom floor at approximately 5:30 PM. Post-fall assessment identified only a skin tear to his right elbow. The response included multiple departures from expected standards:

- [Mrs B] was not informed of the fall
- No pain assessment was conducted
- Neurological observations were performed inconsistently rather than on the required schedule
- No care plan was updated to reflect [Mr A]'s demonstrated functional decline following the fall

Nursing records from subsequent days noted [Mr A] had "poor mobility, pain while weight bearing, and general unwellness," yet no review of the fall was conducted and no corrective actions were implemented by care-home leaders. The clinical adviser characterised the fall management as a moderate departure.

**Documentation Failures**

The clinical adviser observed patterns indicating "a severe disregard for documentation requirements," with care gaps across multiple clinical areas suggesting insufficient nursing oversight and care coordination across [Mr A]'s entire admission.

**Outcome**

[Mr A] was transferred to alternative care closer to his family on 8 May 2024. [Mrs B] stated: "I was given no other option but to send my husband to [Aged Care Facility X]. I am sickened and distressed by the whole experience. I paid for his care and they didn't look after him."

## Gold Standard Findings

### Contributing Factors Identified in Source

- No skin integrity assessment after heel redness first documented
- Two-day delay before bilateral pressure injuries acknowledged
- No wound photography, staging, specialist referral, or repositioning chart
- No family notification of pressure injuries despite EPOA holder available
- No short-term care plan for documented nutritional decline
- 13.6 kg weight loss not escalated to facility leadership
- EPOA not notified of fall
- No pain assessment post-fall
- Neurological observations conducted inconsistently after head-risk fall
- No care plan updated following fall despite documented functional decline
- Systemic severe disregard for documentation requirements

### Root Causes Identified in Source

- Insufficient nursing oversight and care coordination across multiple clinical domains
- Systematic documentation failures reflecting inadequate professional accountability
- Absence of effective escalation mechanisms for deteriorating residents
- No clinical governance systems flagging cumulative deterioration to leadership

### Recommendations from Source

- Written apology to family within three weeks
- Evidence of staff education within three months: pressure injury management, nutritional assessment, falls management, documentation standards
- Wound care training through Health NZ
- Wound assessment protocol alignment with Health NZ standards
- Regular district nursing referrals for wound management
- Senior nurse policy review for wound management, falls, nutritional needs
