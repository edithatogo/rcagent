# Case 03: Multiple System Failures During Six-Week Inpatient Respiratory Admission

**Source**: HDC NZ Decision 21HDC00718 (Aged Care Commissioner, 9 February 2026)
**Event Type**: Medication error (primary) + fall, deterioration, nutritional neglect
**SAC Equivalent**: SAC 2 (significant harm — treatment failure, prolonged deterioration)
**Difficulty**: 3 — Complex

## Case Narrative

[Mr A], a 90-year-old man, was admitted to [Hospital X] on 3 December 2020 with infective bronchiectasis and discharged on 14 January 2021 — a six-week admission marked by multiple concurrent system failures: a dangerous drug interaction not actioned, inappropriate antibiotic prescribing for a known resistant organism, inadequate post-fall neurological monitoring, delayed nutritional support, and insufficient fluid balance monitoring during diuretic therapy.

**Background**

[Mr A] had a pre-existing history of bronchiectasis managed with digoxin and dabigatran, among other medications. In October 2020, [Dr B] at the outpatient respiratory clinic prescribed itraconazole without checking for interactions with [Mr A]'s existing medications. Itraconazole creates dangerous interactions with both digoxin and dabigatran.

**Medication Interaction — Supratherapeutic Digoxin (3–8 December 2020)**

On 4 December 2020, a hospital pharmacist identified the itraconazole-digoxin interaction and made recommendations for digoxin level testing and monitoring for bleeding. [Registrar D] did not order the recommended tests. By 8 December, [Mr A]'s digoxin level was found to be 2.4 — above the therapeutic range of 0.6–2.0. This represented a preventable episode of digoxin toxicity.

On the same day (4 December), [Registrar D] nearly prescribed Clexane while [Mr A] was already taking Pradaxa — the error was caught only because another house officer intervened.

**Antibiotic Stewardship Failure (7–28 December 2020)**

On 7 December, sputum culture identified *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. The Infectious Diseases team appropriately prescribed IV ceftazidime followed by oral ciprofloxacin for a minimum 14-day course per British Thoracic Society guidelines.

However, ceftazidime ceased after one week (14 December) without the transition to oral ciprofloxacin. When [Mr A] deteriorated on 21 December, [Registrar E] prescribed oral Augmentin — a drug the bronchiectasis protocol describes as "not clinically preferred, recommended, or appropriate" for patients with recent Pseudomonas cultures. [Mr A] had previously shown Augmentin resistance.

On 26 December, [House Officer F] escalated to IV Augmentin without adequately reviewing recent sputum cultures. Appropriate anti-Pseudomonal therapy did not resume until 28 December, after further Infectious Diseases consultation.

**Unwitnessed Fall and Inadequate Post-Fall Monitoring (5–6 December 2020)**

[Mr A] fell unwitnessed on the night of 5 December, striking his head. A haematoma on his left forehead was documented on the evening of 6 December. Hospital falls policy required neurological assessments every two hours for 12 hours, then every three hours for 24 hours, then every four hours for a further 24 hours.

Only two neurological assessments were conducted: at 1:40 AM and 4:04 PM on 6 December. No further assessments occurred. The independent advisor described this "paucity of neurological observations" as a severe departure from accepted practice — particularly given that [Mr A] was anticoagulated and the fall was unwitnessed.

[Mr A] also reported left shoulder pain on 7 December. An X-ray was not performed despite continuing pain and family requests. [Health Organisation A] subsequently acknowledged this omission.

**Nutritional Oversight Failure (7 December – 30 December 2020)**

Family raised concerns about [Mr A]'s food intake on 7 December. A care plan was noted requesting a dietician consultation that did not occur until 30 December — a 23-day delay. Weight recording planned for 10 December did not commence until 18 December.

[Mr A] lost approximately 8.4 kg during his six-week stay (from 74 kg to 66 kg). The Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool (MUST) charting indicated seven assessments with consistently low-risk scores, yet only one completed form exists, dated 13 January and apparently filled incorrectly.

**Fluid Balance Monitoring Failure (throughout admission)**

[Mr A] was prescribed frusemide for fluid overload. The Medsafe data sheet for furosemide specifies patients "should be observed for signs of fluid or electrolyte imbalance." During four weeks of diuretic therapy, fluid balance was documented on only five total days across two separate weeks. The Commissioner found this monitoring "clearly inadequate."

**Respiratory Rate Documentation**

[Mr A]'s recorded respiratory rates remained consistently 18–20 per minute throughout his entire six-week admission, including during multiple acute exacerbations. Nursing notes occasionally recorded observations of 30 per minute — inconsistent with the formal records. Both [Mr A]'s daughter and the independent advisor raised this documentation anomaly.

**Expert Opinion and Code Breach**

The Commissioner found [Health Organisation A] (Health NZ Waitematā) breached Right 4(1) of the Code. Multiple gaps in the oversight and coordination of care were identified, including inadequate specialist engagement, incorrect antibiotic prescribing, failure to action a pharmacist drug interaction warning, inadequate post-fall assessment, and insufficient fluid balance monitoring.

Adverse comments were directed at four individual clinicians for failure to read patient notes before prescribing, failure to action pharmacist recommendations, and inappropriate antibiotic selection against a known resistant organism.

## Gold Standard Findings

### Contributing Factors Identified in Source

- Pharmacist drug interaction warning not actioned by prescribing registrar
- Antibiotic ceased prematurely without transition to oral agent
- Inappropriate empirical antibiotic prescribed for known Pseudomonas infection
- Clinician failure to review patient notes before prescribing
- Post-fall neurological monitoring conducted twice (of required ~15 assessments over 48 hours)
- Anticoagulated status not factored into post-fall monitoring intensity
- Dietician referral delayed 23 days after family concern raised
- Fluid balance monitored on only 5 days during 4 weeks of diuretic therapy
- Respiratory rate documentation inconsistency over entire admission
- Absence of referral pathways between General Medicine and Infectious Diseases/Respiratory
- Insufficient oversight of care coordination across specialties

### Root Causes Identified in Source

- No clear referral pathways between General Medicine and specialist services
- Inadequate staff obligations and culture around reviewing patient notes before prescribing
- Electronic drug interaction warnings not consistently actioned
- Absence of bronchiectasis antibiotic guidelines
- No accountability system for nutrition and fluid monitoring during prolonged admissions

### Recommendations from Source

- Written apology to family
- Audit of 30 flagged drug interactions in electronic system for compliance with warnings
- Develop clear referral pathways between General Medicine and Respiratory/Infectious Diseases
- Reinforce obligation to read patient notes and provide care plan directions
- Enhance respiratory rate training (staff to count for full minutes)
- Review anti-Pseudomonal therapy duration guidelines with Infectious Diseases
- Individual practitioner reflections and documented practice changes
