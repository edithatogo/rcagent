# Case 02: Delayed Escalation of Care in Prison Health Leading to Emergency Surgery

**Source**: HDC NZ Decision 22HDC02054 (Health and Disability Commissioner, 16 February 2026)
**Event Type**: Clinical deterioration / failure to rescue
**SAC Equivalent**: SAC 2 (significant harm — emergency surgery, partial colon resection)
**Difficulty**: 2 — Moderate

## Case Narrative

[Ms A], a woman incarcerated at a women's prison, presented with vomiting on 29 June 2022. Over the following six days, nursing staff conducted multiple assessments but failed to document vital signs, perform abdominal examinations, or escalate her deteriorating condition. On 5 July 2022, a medical officer finally ordered blood tests and referred [Ms A] to hospital, where imaging revealed a burst appendix. Emergency surgery on 6 July involved appendectomy, abscess drainage, and partial colon resection.

The Health and Disability Commissioner found the Department of Corrections (through its contracted prison health service) breached the Code of Health and Disability Services Consumers' Rights.

**Initial Presentation (29 June 2022)**

[Ms A] presented to the Health Centre reporting she had been vomiting for three days and was unable to keep food down. Nursing staff assessed this as a likely viral illness and provided symptomatic management. [Ms A] later reported being told to manage her symptoms without further escalation.

**Six-Day Assessment Period (29 June – 5 July 2022)**

Over the following six days, multiple nursing encounters were documented. However, a review of the clinical records revealed critical gaps in assessment and monitoring:

- No vital signs (temperature, blood pressure, pulse, respiratory rate) were recorded at any nursing encounter during this period
- No abdominal examination was documented at any point
- No fluid balance chart was maintained despite [Ms A] experiencing significant and ongoing vomiting
- No systematic bowel movement monitoring was conducted
- Pain assessments were inconsistent and did not use a numerical scale
- Clinical notes at various points contradicted [Ms A]'s reported severity of symptoms

During this period, [Ms A] continued to experience vomiting and abdominal pain. The documented nursing assessments did not reflect the clinical trajectory of a patient with an evolving intra-abdominal emergency.

**Medical Review and Hospital Transfer (5 July 2022)**

On 5 July 2022 — six days after initial presentation — a medical officer reviewed [Ms A] and ordered blood tests. The results prompted immediate referral to the hospital Emergency Department.

Upon hospital admission, imaging confirmed a burst appendix. [Ms A] underwent emergency surgery on 6 July 2022, involving appendectomy, drainage of an abdominal abscess, and partial colon resection. The prolonged delay in diagnosis resulted in significantly greater surgical intervention than would have been required with earlier presentation.

**Staffing Context**

At the time of these events, the prison Health Centre was operating under significant staffing pressures:

- Multiple staff vacancies, with high turnover
- Frequent unplanned staff absences
- No permanent Health Centre Manager in post
- Absence of a Clinical Quality Assurance (CQA) support role

These systemic factors contributed to the absence of clinical oversight that might otherwise have identified [Ms A]'s deteriorating condition.

**Expert Opinion**

Independent clinical advisor [RN B] characterised the nursing failures as severe departures from accepted standards of practice. She identified bowel monitoring as a "basic requirement" for any patient presenting with abdominal pain and vomiting, and noted that the absence of vital signs documentation over six days of repeated nursing contact was inexplicable for a patient with this presentation.

[RN B] noted that the combination of:
- Persistent vomiting over multiple days
- Worsening abdominal symptoms
- Clinical notes inconsistent with patient-reported severity

should have prompted earlier escalation to medical review and/or referral to hospital within the first 48 hours.

**Code Breach Finding**

The HDC found the Department of Corrections breached Right 4(1) of the Code of Health and Disability Services Consumers' Rights — the right to services provided with reasonable care and skill — through:

- Failure to complete abdominal examinations
- Inadequate vital sign monitoring
- Poor documentation practices
- Failure to escalate [Ms A]'s care in light of her symptoms over a six-day period

## Gold Standard Findings

### Contributing Factors Identified in Source

- No vital signs documented over six days of nursing contact
- No abdominal examinations performed
- No fluid balance monitoring despite ongoing vomiting
- No bowel monitoring
- Inconsistent pain assessment without validated tool
- Clinical notes contradicting patient's reported symptom severity
- Multiple staff vacancies and high turnover
- No permanent Health Centre Manager
- Absence of Clinical Quality Assurance support role
- Inadequate triage protocols for deteriorating patients

### Root Causes Identified in Source

- Failure to escalate care in the face of a clinically deteriorating pattern
- Absence of clinical oversight and quality assurance processes
- Inadequate documentation standards for monitoring of deteriorating patients in prison health setting

### Recommendations from Source

- Written apology to [Ms A]
- Staff completion of Graduate Certificate in Rural Assessment of Deteriorating Patient
- Pain management training for nursing staff
- Re-establishment of quality assurance meetings
- Follow-up documentation audits
- Assignment of Clinical Quality Assurance Advisor
- Implementation of updated documentation guidelines (completed 2024)
- New triage procedures
- Peer review meetings for medical officers
