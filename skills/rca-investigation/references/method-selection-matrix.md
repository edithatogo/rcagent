# Method Selection Matrix

## Establish the review process first

Record the jurisdiction, applicable policy and local procedure, review purpose,
scope, commissioning authority and accountable reviewer. Confirm severity,
notification and reporting requirements through that process. Record obligation
triggers and calendars separately from the analytical method; changing a method
does not restart an existing clock.

For Queensland, the [clinical incident management guideline, section 3.2](https://www.health.qld.gov.au/system-governance/policies-standards/health-service-directives/patient-safety/clinical-incident-management)
places SAC 1 method selection within local processes rather than prescribing one
method for every event. The [Patient safety directive](https://www.health.qld.gov.au/system-governance/policies-standards/health-service-directives/patient-safety)
contains applicable obligations; this matrix does not replace them. Verify source
applicability and effective status, including the guideline's conflicting date
metadata. Read the [HEAPS profile](methods/heaps.md) for its source and access
limitations. Do not transplant Queensland requirements into another jurisdiction.

Select HEAPS, RCA or another local review process before choosing tools to answer
its questions. A process name alone does not establish legal protection, severity,
reportability, preventability or authority. In death reviews, keep the separate
incident, mortality, certification and coronial questions visible.

## Select tools by unanswered question

The following are repository planning heuristics, not mandatory bundles,
comparative effectiveness findings or severity-based deadlines. Use the smallest
set that can address the stated questions with available evidence. Read the linked
method guide and check its primary sources and applicability before applying it.

| Unanswered question | Candidate tool | Required contribution and stopping point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What happened, and what remains uncertain about order or timing? | [Timeline analysis](methods/timeline-analysis.md) | Source each observation; retain conflicting accounts, uncertain times and partial order. Do not invent a shared factual sequence. |
| Which working conditions and adaptations warrant investigation? | [Yorkshire Framework](methods/yorkshire-framework.md) or [London Protocol](methods/london-protocol.md) | Use one primary factor/interview structure. Preserve unknown and multiply classified factors; the categories do not establish causation. |
| How did people, tasks, technology, environment and organisation interact? | [SEIPS](methods/seips.md) | Examine evidenced work-system interactions and redesign questions. Stop when further categorisation adds no relevant explanation or testable question. |
| Which safeguards were present, missing, bypassed or ineffective? | [Barrier analysis](methods/barrier-analysis.md), with [Bow-Tie](methods/bow-tie.md) if a hazard/control view is useful | Distinguish observed barrier performance from proposed controls. Record evidence and uncertainty for each connection. |
| Do relevant decisions or conditions span organisational levels? | [AcciMap](methods/accimap.md) | Name the question, actors, relationships, supporting evidence and alternatives. Omit irrelevant levels; graph layout is not proof of causation. |
| Do control constraints and feedback help explain the event? | [STAMP/STPA](methods/stamp-stpa.md) | Specify the control/feedback question and its evidence. Do not add it solely because an AcciMap exists. |
| Does a structured human-factors classification address a remaining question? | [HFACS](methods/hfacs.md) | Trace work conditions and organisational context without converting classification into culpability. Check overlap with the primary analysis. |
| Which possibilities should a team explore before deeper analysis? | [Fishbone](methods/fishbone.md) or [Five Whys](methods/five-whys.md) | Treat entries as candidate questions. Do not force a single cause, stop at individual error, or promote brainstormed entries into findings. |
| What could fail in a proposed service, process or redesign? | [FMEA](methods/fmea.md), optionally with Bow-Tie | Record a prospective scope and assumptions. Prospective risk analysis does not establish what caused a past event. |
| How should evidenced findings become actions and effectiveness checks? | [RCA²](methods/rca-squared.md) and the action templates | Link each recommendation to its condition/finding, mechanism, owner and measures. Keep implementation separate from effectiveness. |

## Manage overlap consistently

- **London Protocol and Yorkshire Framework:** normally choose one primary
  structure for factor identification. Use an element of the other only when a
  distinct question remains and the mapping avoids duplicate findings. There is
  no universal ban or automatic requirement to use both.
- **HFACS and AcciMap:** choose according to the question and evidence, not a SAC
  label. If both are used, explain the distinct contribution and retain common
  canonical references rather than counting the same observation twice.
- **AcciMap and STAMP/STPA:** distinguish the question about cross-level conditions
  from the question about control and feedback. Use both only if each adds a
  necessary view supported by evidence.
- **Barrier analysis and Bow-Tie:** reuse the same barrier/evidence identities.
  A differently drawn control is not an additional independent finding.

## Record a selection and stop decision

Before adding a tool, record the unanswered question, required evidence, expected
output, overlap with existing work and responsible reviewer. After using it,
record what changed: evidence gaps, alternatives, findings, proposed actions or
confidence. A useful result can confirm uncertainty or prompt abstention; it need
not create another finding or recommendation.

Stop or narrow the analysis when the question is answered to the available
evidence, when additional methods repeat the same content, or when missing
evidence prevents a defensible answer. Keep the gap and its consequence visible.
Do not convert a budget or deadline into pressure to fill unknown fields. Escalate
outstanding safety or authority questions through the applicable local process.

Use the [combination guide](method-combination-guide.md) for worked planning
patterns. No method count in this repository determines investigation quality.
